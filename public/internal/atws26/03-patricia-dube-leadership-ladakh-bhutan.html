Itinerary 1 — Ladakh · The challenge format

For groups that want to be tested physically as well as intellectually. 13 days door to door, 10 on the ground.

Season: June, July or August only. Spring and autumn are out; winter is impassable. This is the opposite of Bhutan's calendar and it constrains her scheduling more than anything else in this document.

The argument

Ladakh is extreme, but the extremity comes from altitude, not from technical difficulty. Nothing here requires a climber. What it requires is patience, and that is the point — the mountain enforces the slowing down she spends the whole trip teaching.

Shape

Days Where Leadership frame
1 Delhi arrival, met at the airport, hotel —
2 Fly Delhi → Leh. Transfer to Nimmu. Arrival, contracting, group norms
3–4 Acclimatisation at Nimmu, based in a converted Ladakhi noble homestead Enforced slowness. Short walks, long sessions. The altitude does the teaching.
5–8 Zanskar Valley — day hikes, one multi-day option, palace ruins, curated homestays Discomfort with purpose. Sessions after dinner, on the floor, with the host family's household around
9 Non-technical summit option or valley day The choice itself is the exercise
10 Return to Leh Integration
11 Leh buffer day Held for weather
12–13 Leh → Delhi → home —

Two options every day

Days 5 to 9 each carry a harder and a gentler version, running from the same start point and rejoining at the same overnight. Nobody is left at the hotel and nobody is held back. For a group of owners with a wide fitness spread, this is the design decision that makes the trip work.

What Ladakh gives a leadership programme that a conference room cannot

  • Valley floors above 11,000 ft; road passes at 17,500 and up.
  • Homestays that are functioning family homes — meals on the floor, with the family, not a trekker circuit.
  • A landscape that makes people feel small in the useful sense: small and exactly in the right place.

Itinerary 2 — Bhutan · The reflective format

For groups that need quiet, solitude and a closed environment. 13 days door to door, 10 on the ground.

Season: March–May or September–November.

The argument

Bhutan has a quietude the other Himalayan countries do not. More to the point for her: the lodge can be closed to her group entirely.

Why the buyout is the product

With 12 to 25 people and enough notice, we close the property. Everyone on site is her group, for five days. No other guests, no other programme, no drift.

Inside that:

  • Dedicated programme space — built in, because most Bhutanese properties have rooms and a restaurant and nowhere to actually run a session.
  • A chef's table with the lead chefs cooking with and for the group. Raised in the room as an example of the kind of experience that becomes a leadership exercise rather than a meal.
  • Hot stone baths and active recovery, so a harder day does not cost the following morning.
  • A 15-acre working farm — the food arrives from the field to the kitchen with nothing in between. For a group spending two weeks on alignment and slowing down, the farm is a teaching object, not scenery.
  • Harder and gentler activity options straight off the property.
