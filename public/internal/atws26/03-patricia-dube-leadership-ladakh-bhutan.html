Who this is for

Your clients are owners, and they have already heard everything you have to say

You run leadership and self-growth travel for professionals and entrepreneurs — that is the whole business, not a line of it. Your groups are a dozen or more business owners who register as individuals, travelling with people from unrelated companies, working on values, communication, stress and alignment.

You named the commercial hinge yourself, and it is the reason this document is shaped the way it is: when the training comes from someone other than you, the trip becomes fully deductible for your clients — and a different voice opens something yours no longer can, because your people have heard you already.

The Himalaya is new to you. You have taken groups to Japan and Vietnam. So both itineraries below are written to be legible without having stood in the place.

Why us rather than a ground operator plus a trainer

Brian can deliver both halves, which is unusual and is the point

Adventure Access is the ground operation: permits, transport, guides, lodging, one point of contact. That part any competent DMC claims.

The part that is not standard: Brian has done executive coaching professionally, sat on the ATTA education team — including the team that trained the Japan National Tourism Bureau ahead of ATWS there — and has worked with Columbia University and the US State Department’s Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs, coaching leaders both in the United States and out of the Himalaya.

That means the logistics and the training can come from the same supplier, and your clients’ trips become deductible. If you would rather take the logistics only and bring your own leader, that is entirely fine — both itineraries below work unchanged, and it is the arrangement you use in Tanzania today.

Ladakh — the challenge format

For groups that want to be tested physically as well as intellectually.

On the ground10 days

SeasonJune · July · August
Valley floorAbove 11,000 ft

Ladakh is extreme, but the extremity comes from altitude, not technical difficulty. Nothing on this route requires a climber. What it requires is patience — which is precisely the thing you spend the trip teaching.

The season constrains you more than anything else in this document. June, July and August only. Spring and autumn are out, winter is impassable. That is the exact opposite of Bhutan’s calendar, so the two itineraries cannot be run in the same window and should be scheduled as opposite halves of a year.

The flight in is short and the altitude gain is immediate, which is why nothing is planned for the afternoon. Evening session: arrival, contracting, group norms. The leadership frame writes itself here. You spend your programmes teaching people to slow down; in Ladakh the altitude does it for you and there is no negotiating with it. These are not dead days to be endured before the trip starts. They are the most productive session time on the whole itinerary, because the mountain has already made your argument.

What Ladakh gives a leadership programme that a room cannot

  • Valley floors above 11,000 ft, with road passes at 17,500 and higher.
  • Homestays that are working family homes — meals on the floor, with the family, not a trekker circuit dressed up as one.
  • A landscape that makes people feel small in the useful sense: small, and exactly where they should be.
Photography

Our Ladakh library is almost entirely one mountain-biking trip — riders in full-face helmets on loose scree, none of which belongs anywhere near this itinerary. The frames on this page are the handful that carry no bikes. We hold nothing at all of a homestay interior, a session on the floor, or a group of adults in a Ladakhi kitchen, which are the three images that would actually sell this trip. That is a gap in our photography and not a gap in the programme.

Why the buyout is the product, not an upgrade

With 12 to 25 people and enough notice we close the property. For five days everyone on site is your group. No other guests, no other programme, no drift, nobody at the next table. For work that depends on people being willing to say uncomfortable things out loud, that is not a luxury — it is the operating condition.

Your 18-month planning cycle and a buyout requirement fit each other exactly, which is not true of most properties worth buying out.

Inside that

  • Dedicated programme space, built in. Most Bhutanese properties have rooms and a restaurant and nowhere to actually run a session.
  • A chef’s table with the lead chefs cooking with and for the group — the kind of thing that becomes a leadership exercise rather than a meal.
  • Hot stone baths and active recovery, so a harder day does not cost you the following morning. These are a centuries-old farmers’ practice here, not a spa invention.
  • A 15-acre working farm. Food arrives from the field to the kitchen with nothing in between. For a group spending two weeks on alignment and slowing down, the farm is a teaching object rather than scenery.
  • Harder and gentler activity options straight off the property, chosen that morning.
