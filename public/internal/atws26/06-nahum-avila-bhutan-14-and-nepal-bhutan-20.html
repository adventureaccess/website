Itinerary 1 — Bhutan · 14 days

14 days door to door, 11 on the ground. Four days go to travel at each end of an Asia trip, which is why 12 is the floor for this market.

Season: March–May or September–November. Monsoon rules out the summer.

Day 1–2 — In transit. Québec City to Bhutan via Montréal or Toronto and one Asian hub.

Day 3 — Arrive Paro (7,300 ft). Met at the airport. Short transfer, easy afternoon, nothing scheduled.

Day 4 — Paro valley. Kyichu Lhakhang, the valley floor, a short walk. Gentle first full day on purpose.

Day 5 — Tiger's Nest. The one image everyone in the group will already have in their head. A real climb but a well-graded trail, and the group splits naturally — full ascent, the halfway teahouse, or the valley below. Nobody is excluded.

Day 6 — Paro → Thimphu. 90 minutes. The takin preserve, the weekend market if the day falls right, the capital at its own pace.

Day 7 — Thimphu → Punakha over Dochu La. The pass at 10,200 ft with the eastern Himalaya behind the chortens on a clear morning. Down to Punakha at 4,300 ft, which is warm. Punakha Dzong at the confluence of the two rivers.

Day 8 — Punakha. River valley, birding, suspension bridge, village walking. Active but flat.

Day 9 — Punakha → the lodge. About an hour. This is our own property — 20 keys, a 15-acre working farm, adjacent to a protected Ramsar wetland, off the standard circuit. Afternoon free; traditional hot stone bath in the evening.

Day 10 — Lodge. Two options offered each morning: a longer hike into the hills, or the farm, the village and the valley floor. The group splits and rejoins at dinner.

Day 11 — Phobjikha. The wider valley, Gangtey, the flat nature trail. In late autumn this is where the black-necked cranes are — they winter here from late October into March.

Day 12 — Lodge → Paro, with stops. Last evening in the Paro valley.

Day 13 — Paro buffer. Held deliberately. Weather disruption is the normal failure mode and a buffer day is cheaper than a missed international connection.

Day 14 — Depart.

Itinerary 2 — Kathmandu and Bhutan · 20 days

20 days door to door, 17 on the ground. Built the way his Ecuador trip is built: two places whose difference is the point.

The argument to sell it with: Kathmandu is a spiritual explosion; Bhutan is quiet. Neither one lands the same way without the other. The contrast is the value, and the 45-minute Kathmandu–Paro flight along the Himalayan wall is one of the great flights anywhere.

Nepal — days 3 to 9

Day 3 — Arrive Kathmandu. Met, transferred, nothing scheduled.

Day 4 — Kathmandu valley. Boudhanath, Patan, the old city. Dense, loud, extraordinary.

Day 5 — Out of the valley to the hills. The transition from city to terraces happens faster than anyone expects.

Days 6–8 — Three walking days, village to village, at soft-adventure pace: five to six hours a day, no camping, comfortable lodging, mountain views from the ridges. Off the Everest and Annapurna circuits entirely — those are crowded, and Annapurna now has road noise on parts of it.

Day 9 — Return to Kathmandu. Afternoon free.

Bhutan — days 10 to 19

Day 10 — Kathmandu → Paro. The flight. Book the left-hand side.

Days 11–18 — As days 4 to 12 of Itinerary 1, compressed by one day: Paro, Tiger's Nest, Thimphu, Dochu La, Punakha, three nights at the lodge with the split-option days, Phobjikha, back to Paro.

Day 19 — Paro buffer.

Day 20 — Depart.
