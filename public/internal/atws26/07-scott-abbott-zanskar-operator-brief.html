Why there is no itinerary in here

You sell almost everywhere we operate, so the interesting question is not what we offer

Wilderness Travel runs Bhutan, Nepal, Tibet and India already. If we sent you a specimen itinerary you would read it as us explaining your own region back to you, and you would be right to.

So the honest framing is the one from the table: not would you sell what we have, but what operational gaps could we fill. This document answers that and nothing else.

The short version: Zanskar, our own crews rather than contracted ones, permits held rather than brokered, and — from next year — a 20-key property in central Bhutan that is not on any booking engine.

The gap

Zanskar, rather than Ladakh as everyone routes it

Ladakh sells itself along one corridor: Leh, the monasteries on the Indus, north to the famous lake, and the photograph everyone already owns. That is where the domestic traffic goes, and increasingly where the international traffic goes too.

Zanskar is west, and it is quiet. Palace ruins reached on a day hike, homestays that are functioning family homes rather than a circuit, and valley floors above 11,000 ft where the difficulty comes from altitude rather than technicality — which is a different, and considerably more saleable, kind of hard.

For a company whose clients have already done the Indus corridor, Zanskar is the second Ladakh trip that currently does not exist in most catalogues.

The property, and why it is relevant to you specifically

You already sell Bhutan, so the destination is not the pitch. The property might be.

  • 20 keys — 16 standard rooms, one two-bedroom villa, two one-bedroom villas. Small enough that a group of yours could take the whole thing.
  • Central Bhutan, in the valley between Phobjikha and Wangdue, about an hour from Punakha and three from the capital. We are the only rooms in that valley, which is the part that is hard to replicate.
  • A 15-acre working farm adjacent to a protected Ramsar wetland. Farm-to-table with no transport step. Full spa, traditional hot stone baths, dedicated programme space.
  • Three partners: Brian, a Bhutanese partner, and a long-standing ATTA figure based in Kathmandu.
Two things to say plainly

It is not open yet — soft opening early spring, fully operational by summer, autumn bookings underway. And it has no decided name, which is why nothing in this document names it. You asked directly at the table and the honest answer has not changed since.
