What this is, and what it deliberately is not

No route, because you would be right to judge us on one

You have been running bicycle tours for more than fifty years. If we sent you a day-by-day for a country we have not yet ridden end to end with a loaded group, you would see through it in a paragraph, and rightly.

So there is no route in this document. The route comes after Brian has ridden it, and he is resident in Bhutan from late October. What is here instead is everything that determines whether a route is possible at all: the season, the base, the bikes, the air access, the evacuation plan, and the honest list of what is not solved.

On your side the shape is already clear. Beyond is e-bike only for now, rides as a group rather than independently, runs seven to nine days on location, and gets two departures per destination. Your preferred cadence is one in May or June and one in late September or October, and we are talking about 2029.

Why Bhutan and not Ladakh or Nepal

One of the three is a cycling destination and the other two are not

Ladakh is the extreme-altitude option and its road season is June to August only — which collides with half your cadence and leaves no second window.

Nepal is better on foot than on wheels. That is a statement about the standard product and the road surfaces, not about the country.

Bhutan is the fit, and it is where our base, our lodge and Brian himself will be. It also runs two seasons rather than one, which is the only way your May/June and late-September cadence works in a single destination.

March – MayPeak, spring
Your first departure lands here. May yes, June marginal — June is the front edge of the rains.
September – NovemberPeak, autumn · best clear skies
Your second departure. Late September into the first half of October is the strongest fortnight of the year.

June to August is monsoon and is out. December to February is cold but workable at valley altitude and dry — not proposed here, but not impossible if you ever wanted a third.

Two

Bikes on the ground — the real constraint

There is no bike shop in Bhutan. Not a poor one: none.

Sourcing in countryNone
Our planContainer via India
Our own workshopUS retail + service
E-bike experienceYours, not ours

You cannot source bikes, parts or batteries inside Bhutan. Everything arrives or it does not exist, and that single fact shapes the whole operation.

  • Our plan is to ship a container of bikes in, routed through India — all port access to Bhutan runs through India, and that is where the government relationships sit.
  • We already run a full retail and service centre in the US, so mechanical standards and fleet management are in-house rather than outsourced to whoever is available.
  • On e-bikes specifically, your experience should lead this conversation rather than ours. ExperiencePlus shipped the first commercial e-bike into Jordan. We have not shipped commercial e-bikes internationally. Saying otherwise would be the fastest way to lose your confidence.
Not solved, and not claimed as solved

Battery import and the paperwork around it. Air-freight restrictions on lithium. Charging infrastructure on a rural Bhutanese route. Spares depth for a group ride three hours from the capital. None of these have answers yet. They are all answerable, and none of them should be answered by guessing.

You already see this with Tanzania safari extensions: guests add on before or after, and the operator who runs the main trip should not be the one improvising the edges.

  • Front end: Kathmandu, two or three days, including the 45-minute Paro flight along the Himalayan wall.
  • Back end: trekking days, cultural days, or a deeper eastern Bhutan loop.
  • Priced and operated separately, sold through you or handed to us direct — whichever you prefer.
