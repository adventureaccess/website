Starting from what you actually said

You did not request the meeting, and you took it anyway

You said so in the first minute, and you were candid about why: you normally write to cancel when there is no possible fit, and this time you did not. You were curious about what we do there.

More of your clients ask you for something else each year, and you have always said no — this is what we do, it is all we can offer. You are beginning to wonder whether this region is something you might one day consider. That is a long way from wanting to buy Himalayan product, and this document does not pretend otherwise.

What follows is a one-page reference for the day a client asks you what else, plus the thing you raised yourself and which is worth more than anything we could sell you.

The shape of this

Different cold, the same problem

You moved to Greenland in 2001 and lived there the best part of ten years. Brian moved to Tibet in 2001. Neither of you needed to explain the rest to the other: what it takes to build an operation in a place where you have to teach every single step, because there is no chain to plug into.

Arctic Yeti is exclusively Nordic and you were clear that Iceland, Greenland, the Faroes, Norway, Finland and Sweden are not variations on each other. That is exactly our experience of Ladakh against Bhutan, and it is why neither of us can be usefully described as a regional generalist.

And the client at the far end of both funnels is the same person — the one who asks where else, and who do you trust there. This is a connection to keep in the pocket and pull out when the opportunity comes. Not a campaign.

The guide exchange — your ask, not ours

The item most likely to produce something real inside a year, and it costs a phone call.

You raisedThe ATTA guide standard
Your guidesSeasonal, and good
Our seasonsClose to complementary
Cost to tryOne conversation

This is the part you initiated, and it should be treated as the most serious thing in this document.

  • You have met a number of companies through the ATTA guide standard and you exchange guides across seasons with them. Many of your guides are seasonal and very good, and a network of trustworthy people who fit each other’s programmes is worth having for its own sake.
  • We have been part of ATTA for many years, and Brian has been on the education side — including the team ATTA ran with the Japan National Tourism Bureau.
  • Our operations are hungry for the guide standard. Adventure Access runs two sides: one develops entrepreneurs, operators and guides across the region, the other is client-facing. The development side is what makes the client-facing side trustworthy, because we built the system rather than bought it in.
  • Our seasonal shape and yours are close to complementary, which is the precondition for an exchange actually working rather than sounding good.

What we would propose: one conversation on how your exchange network works in practice — what a placement looks like, what you expect of a visiting guide, and what each side’s seasons really allow. Nothing to sign, and nothing to sell.

Said plainly

A seasonal placement carries real obligations in both directions, and we would rather work out whether we can meet them before proposing it than after.
