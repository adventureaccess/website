What she told us

  • Beyond is their new style and the only one that fits a destination like this: going past traditional cycling-tour geography. Current Beyond destinations are Tanzania, Jordan and Arctic Finland.
  • Beyond is e-bike only for now. "Anything can change" — but that is today's answer.
  • Beyond rides as a group. Their classic tours let guests ride independently through the day; in Tanzania or the Himalaya that is not advisable, and they know it.
  • Two departures per destination, and next year's are already sold out. Medium-sized company, large destination list, high repeat rate, so each destination gets two or three departures and no more.
  • Trip length seven to nine days on location, with guests adding extensions front or back. They do not want to build or run the extensions — they would hand those to us.
  • Her preferred cadence: one departure in May or June, one in late September or October.
  • We are talking about 2029.
  • She knows bikes are hard in this region: ExperiencePlus shipped the first commercial e-bike into Jordan themselves.

The one-line answer to "why Bhutan rather than Ladakh or Nepal"

Ladakh is the extreme altitude option and its road season is June to August only. Nepal is better on foot than on wheels. Bhutan is the fit, and it is where our base, our lodge and Brian himself will be.

1. Season

Bhutan runs two seasons because of the monsoon.

Window Status Fits her cadence?
March – May Peak, spring Her May/June departure: May yes, June marginal
June – August Monsoon. Out. No
September – November Peak, fall; best clear skies Her late-Sept/October departure: yes
December – February Cold but workable at valley altitude, dry Not proposed

Recommendation: mid-to-late May, and late September into the first half of October. Her instinct of "May or June" should land on May; June is the front edge of the rains.

2. The lodge as the anchor

The plan is to build the route through the lodge rather than out of a single base:

  • 20 keys — 16 standard rooms plus one two-bedroom and two one-bedroom villas. A Beyond group of their size fits.
  • 15-acre working farm; kitchen is farm-to-table with no transport step in between.
  • Full spa, traditional hot stone baths, and active-recovery massage. For a cycling group four days into a route at altitude, that is the actual argument, not a nice-to-have.
  • Roughly three hours' drive from the capital, about an hour from Punakha. Off the main tourist circuit.
  • It is not open yet. Soft opening early spring, fully operational by summer, fall normal. For a 2029 departure that is a non-issue; for anything earlier it is not.

3. Bikes on the ground — the real constraint

There is no bike shop in Bhutan. You cannot source bikes, parts or batteries in country.

  • Our plan is to ship a container of bikes in, routed through India — all port access to Bhutan runs through India, and that is where the government relationships sit.
  • We already run a full retail and service centre in the US, so mechanical standards and fleet management are in-house rather than outsourced.
  • E-bikes specifically: ExperiencePlus has shipped commercial e-bikes internationally before and we have not. This is the single item where their experience should lead the conversation rather than ours.
  • Open item: battery import, air-freight restrictions on lithium, charging infrastructure on a rural route, and spares depth for a group ride. None of these are solved and none should be claimed as solved.

4. Air access and arrival

One international airport, at Paro. Practical routings for their guests:

  • Bangkok — easiest
  • Singapore — easy, further south; makes sense for anyone coming via Australia
  • Delhi — access point, but a difficult transit for guests
  • Kathmandu — access point, not a good transit, but a strong front-end extension
  • Dubai — direct twice weekly, paused when the war started; restart flagged for November but seats not yet on sale

By 2029 Bhutan's flight infrastructure is likely to have moved; the country is investing in it.

We meet guests at Paro, carry permits, all domestic transport and the whole ground operation from arrival to departure.

5. Safety and evacuation

  • Serious care goes to the capital.
  • Anything beyond in-country capability goes to Bangkok, which functions as the regional equivalent of flying someone to London or New York.
  • Helicopter service exists, and one of the three lodge partners owns the helicopters.
  • Medical planning, permits and transport are all carried by us, not by them.

6. The extension question

She expects guests to add on before or after — as they already see with Tanzania safari extensions — and she said plainly they do not want to be the ones running them. That is ours:

  • Front end: Kathmandu, two or three days, including the 45-minute Paro flight along the Himalaya.
  • Back end: trekking days, cultural days, or a deeper eastern Bhutan loop.
  • Priced and operated separately, sold through them or handed to us direct — her call.
