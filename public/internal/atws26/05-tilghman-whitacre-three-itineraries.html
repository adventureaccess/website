What she told us

  • She walked the Manaslu Circuit this spring, specifically to avoid the crowds. She loved it. Restricted-area rules mean a guide is mandatory, which keeps the numbers down, and by day two she felt she was in a different world — supplies moving by donkey, nothing else.
  • Her question, almost verbatim: what else in that region can I sell that is not Everest Base Camp or Annapurna? She has heard Annapurna now has road noise on it.
  • She sends people away from creature comforts on purpose: "Don't just go somewhere you'll find the same things you have in America."
  • On guides, she already gets it. Her Manaslu guide was hard work on day one and she is still texting him on Instagram. She understands a guide as the experience rather than a requirement.
  • She has been telling clients who want a spiritual trip to go to Bhutan rather than Nepal, and wanted confirmation she was right. She is.
  • She is dying to go to Bhutan. She was invited on a Bhutan fam and it turned out to be four weeks away rather than next year, so she had to decline. Brian invited her to come see us.

How these are written

She is a retail advisor, not an operator. She does not need day-by-day operational depth — she needs to be able to tell a client, in one paragraph, why this trip and not the famous one. So each itinerary below leads with the sentence she can repeat, then the shape underneath it.

Every itinerary here is fully customisable. There is no fixed departure to join.

Nepal — two answers that are not Everest Base Camp

We do take people to Everest Base Camp. We try to talk them elsewhere first.

1. The circuit she already knows, sold properly

The sentence: "You get the Himalaya without the queue, and the permit rules are the reason why."

She has walked this. That makes it the easiest thing on this list for her to sell, because she can speak from her own legs. Where we add: restricted-area permits held by us, our own crew rather than a rostered stranger, and the acclimatisation profile built rather than assumed.

Shape: 14–16 days door to door. Kathmandu in, trek, Kathmandu out, with two buffer days that most operators quietly skip.

2. The quieter valleys, for the client who cannot walk for two weeks

The sentence: "Same mountains, same culture, half the walking, none of the crowd."

For clients who want Himalayan Nepal without a full circuit: shorter walking days, village-to-village, cultural depth carrying the load rather than distance. Not a trek with the hard parts removed — a different trip.

Shape: 10–12 days door to door.

What we will not do: put her clients into the standard lodge circuit and call it bespoke. Community structures there equalise the product deliberately — same menu, same rooms, and local families are discouraged from standing out. It is culturally driven and it is not fixable by an operator; it is avoidable by routing.

Ladakh — the one nobody in her network is selling

The sentence: "It is Tibetan Buddhist India, at altitude, and the good part is the valley the crowds skip."

Why it is different from Nepal

  • Zanskar Valley, west, quiet. Domestic tourism runs north and east to the famous lake and the famous photograph.
  • Ancient culture rather than big-mountain base camp. Palace ruins on day hikes.
  • Homestays that are functioning family homes. She will understand the distinction immediately after Manaslu: on the trekking circuit you are a trekker; here you are invited to a family's table for the days you are there.
  • Altitude does the pacing. Two real acclimatisation days at Nimmu before anything begins, because skipping them ruins trips.

Season: June, July, August only. That is a booking fact for her — it is the opposite of Bhutan.

Shape: 12 days door to door, 9 on the ground. Delhi received by our own office, Delhi–Leh flight managed by us, Nimmu, Zanskar, Leh, buffer day.

For her specifically: she sells to clients who want rugged first and comfort afterwards. Ladakh is built exactly that way here — comfortable entry, purposeful roughness in the middle, comfortable exit.

Bhutan — where she is right, and why

The sentence: "Nepal is a spiritual explosion. Bhutan is quiet. Send the seeker to Bhutan."

She has been giving clients this advice already. It is correct, and now she can give it with a supplier behind it.

  • Bhutan has a quietude none of the others have, and it is not manufactured for visitors.
  • Comfortable and flexible: soft days and hard days from the same base, chosen that morning.
  • Season: March–May and September–November. Complementary to Ladakh, which matters for an advisor filling a year.
  • Our own 20-key property opens in central Bhutan — 16 rooms and three villas, a 15-acre working farm, full spa, traditional hot stone baths, adjacent to a protected wetland, about an hour from Punakha and off the standard circuit. Small, ours, and not on any booking engine. For an advisor whose clients want the finer things after the trail, that is the trade she cannot get elsewhere.

Shape: 10–12 days on the ground. Paro, Thimphu, Dochu La to Punakha, several nights at the lodge, Phobjikha, out via Paro.

The multi-country argument — worth more to her than any single itinerary

Kathmandu is one of only five access points into Bhutan. The others are Delhi, Bangkok, Singapore and one further Indian city; the Dubai flight came online and paused when the war started.

So a Nepal client is already one 45-minute flight from Bhutan — a flight that runs along the Himalayan wall past Everest, on a smaller prop aircraft if you book it right.

Even a weekend in Kathmandu on the front or back of a Bhutan trip is worth selling, and not as a warm-up. The contrast is the value: you understand each country better for having felt the other. For an advisor, it is also a longer, higher-value booking for the same client conversation.
