Who this is for

You have already done the hard version, so this skips the persuading

You walked the Manaslu Circuit this spring specifically to avoid the crowds, and by the second day you were somewhere the supply chain still moves by donkey. You did not come to our table to be sold the Himalaya. You came with a sharper question: what else in that region can I sell that is not Everest Base Camp or Annapurna?

So this is not written the way we would write for an operator. You do not need our day-by-day; you need the paragraph you can say to a client that makes them choose the trip nobody else offered them. Each of the three below leads with that sentence, then gives the shape underneath it.

Everything here is fully customisable. We run no set departures, so there is no fixed trip for a client to join and nothing to fit them into.

The circuit you already know, sold properly

“You get the Himalaya without the queue, and the permit rules are the reason why.”

You have walked this, which makes it the easiest thing on this list for you to sell — you can speak from your own legs rather than from a brochure. What we add is underneath the walking: restricted-area permits held by us rather than brokered, our own crew rather than a guide rostered on the morning you arrive, and an acclimatisation profile that is built rather than assumed.

Shape: 14–16 days door to door. Kathmandu in, trek, Kathmandu out, with two buffer days that most operators quietly leave out.

The quieter valleys, for the client who cannot walk for two weeks

“Same mountains, same culture, half the walking, none of the crowd.”

For clients who want Himalayan Nepal without committing to a full circuit: shorter walking days, village to village, with cultural depth carrying the trip rather than distance. This is not a trek with the hard parts removed. It is a different trip, and it should be sold as one.

Shape: 10–12 days door to door.

What we will not do

We will not put your clients into the standard lodge circuit and call it bespoke. The community structures along those trails deliberately equalise the product — the same menu, the same rooms, and local families discouraged from standing out. That is culturally driven, it is not an operator’s failing, and it cannot be fixed by trying harder. It can only be avoided by routing somewhere else.

Photography

Our Nepal library is Everest-region trekking and Boudhanath, and nothing in between. There is no Manaslu in it at all, and the trekking frames we do hold show full expedition packs, which oversells both routes above. The images in this section are Kathmandu and one generic ridge. That is a gap in our photography, not a gap in the trip.

“It is Tibetan Buddhist India, at altitude, and the good part is the valley the crowds skip.”

Why it is a different sell from Nepal

  • Zanskar is west, and quiet. Domestic tourism runs north and east, toward the famous lake and the photograph everyone has seen.
  • Ancient culture rather than base camp. Palace ruins reached on a day hike, not an expedition.
  • Homestays that are functioning family homes. After Manaslu you will understand the distinction immediately: on a trekking circuit you are a trekker passing through; here you are at a family’s table for as long as you stay.
  • Altitude sets the pace. Two real acclimatisation days at Nimmu before anything begins, because skipping them is how trips get ruined.

The season is the booking fact

June, July and August only. Spring and autumn are out and winter is impassable — which is the exact opposite of Bhutan. For an advisor trying to fill a year, that complementarity is worth more than either destination on its own.

Shape

12 days door to door, 9 on the ground. Delhi received by our own office, the Delhi–Leh flight managed by us, two nights at Nimmu, five days in Zanskar, back to Leh, and a buffer day held deliberately.

You said you send people away from creature comforts on purpose. Ladakh is built that way here without being punishing: comfortable entry, purposeful roughness in the middle, comfortable exit.

The frames below are the village, monastery and river images that carry no bikes. They are the right images, but there are only a handful of them, and there is nothing at all of the homestay interiors that are the best argument for this trip.

“Nepal is a spiritual explosion. Bhutan is quiet. Send the seeker to Bhutan.”

Bhutan has a quietude none of the others have, and it is not manufactured for visitors. Monastery mornings are not a scheduled experience; they are Tuesday. That is the whole difference, and it is why your instinct was right before you had anyone to book it with.

What the trip is like

  • Comfortable and flexible — soft days and harder days available from the same base, chosen that morning rather than locked in at booking.
  • Season: March to May and September to November. Complementary to Ladakh, which matters when you are filling a calendar.
  • Our own 20-key property opens in central Bhutan: 16 rooms and three villas on a 15-acre working farm, full spa, traditional hot stone baths, adjacent to a protected wetland, about an hour from Punakha and off the standard circuit. Small, ours, and not on any booking engine. For an advisor whose clients want the finer things after the trail, that is the trade nobody else can offer you.

Shape: 10–12 days on the ground. Paro, Thimphu, over Dochu La to Punakha, several nights at the lodge, Phobjikha, out via Paro.

The property is not open yet

Soft opening in early spring, fully operational by summer, with fall bookings underway. It also does not have a decided name, so nothing here gives it one. Say both things to a client rather than letting them assume it is bookable tomorrow.

The argument worth more than any single itinerary

Kathmandu is one of only five ways into Bhutan

The others are Delhi, Bangkok, Singapore and one further Indian city. The Dubai route came online and paused when the war started; a November restart was mentioned but seats are not on sale.

Which means a Nepal client is already one 45-minute flight from Bhutan — and that flight runs along the Himalayan wall past Everest, on a smaller prop aircraft if it is booked deliberately rather than accepted.

Even a weekend in Kathmandu on the front or back of a Bhutan trip is worth selling, and not as a warm-up. The contrast is the value: each country lands harder for having felt the other. For you it is also a longer, higher-value booking out of the same client conversation.
