Why this is a specimen and not a proposal

You asked to see how we build, not what we sell

Privada is private travel for discerning explorers, curated rather than packaged. You do not need a catalogue from us and we do not have one to give you — we run no set departures at all. Everything is built for the client in front of us.

So the itinerary below is a specimen. It is a real route we can operate, written at the level of detail we would actually deliver, and its purpose is to let you judge the construction rather than the destination: where we put the slack, what we refuse to compress, and what we tell a client they cannot have.

Your market already sells Asia and touches India. What it almost certainly does not sell is Zanskar, and that is the part of this worth your attention.

What to notice about the construction

  • Two acclimatisation days, not one. They are the first thing a competitor cuts to make a price look better, and the first thing your client will feel.
  • A buffer day at the end, not the beginning. Held against the flight out of Leh, which is the leg that actually fails.
  • Five nights in one valley rather than a circuit. Private clients almost never want to move every night; they want somewhere to come back to.
  • Our own office in Delhi. The arrival is the part of a Himalayan trip most often handed to a stranger.
Season is the hard constraint

June, July and August only. Spring and autumn are out; winter is impassable. That is the opposite of Bhutan, which is useful — between the two we can fill most of a year for the same client.

Photography

Our Ladakh library is almost entirely one mountain-biking trip: riders in full-face helmets on loose scree, none of which belongs in a private cultural itinerary. The images here are the handful that carry no bikes. We hold nothing of the homestay interiors or the noble house at Nimmu, which are the two things that would sell this trip fastest. That is a gap in our photography, not in the product.

The second half of the answer

Bhutan, which is where the same client goes next

Ladakh and Bhutan run in opposite seasons and feel nothing like each other, which makes them a natural pair for a client who comes back rather than a choice between two similar trips.

Bhutan is the comfortable one: quieter, warmer at valley altitude, and flexible day to day — soft days and harder days available from the same base, chosen that morning rather than locked in months earlier. For a private client that flexibility is usually worth more than any single sight.

And we have our own property there. Twenty keys in central Bhutan — 16 rooms and three villas on a 15-acre working farm, full spa, traditional hot stone baths, adjacent to a protected wetland, about an hour from Punakha and off the standard circuit. Small, ours, and on no booking engine. For an operator selling private travel, an address nobody else can book is a different kind of asset from a good hotel.
